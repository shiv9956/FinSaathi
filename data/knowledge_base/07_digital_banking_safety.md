# Digital Banking Safety — PIN, OTP, and Account Security

## The Golden Rule

**Never share your OTP, UPI PIN, ATM PIN, CVV, card number, or internet banking password with ANYONE — including people claiming to be from your bank, NPCI, RBI, or the government.**

Legitimate bank employees and government officials will NEVER ask for these details.

## What is an OTP and How is it Used?

An OTP (One-Time Password) is a temporary 4-8 digit code sent to your registered mobile number to verify a transaction or login. It is valid for a short time (usually 5-10 minutes).

**Safe use:** Enter OTP only on the official website or app you are already logged into.
**Never:** Read out your OTP to anyone on the phone or type it on any site you reached through a link in an SMS/email.

## ATM and Debit Card Safety

- Never let anyone see you enter your ATM PIN — shield the keypad with your hand.
- Do not write your PIN on your card or wallet.
- Check the card slot and keypad for unusual devices (card skimmers) before using an ATM.
- After completing a transaction, wait for the machine to eject your card before walking away.
- Set up SMS alerts for every transaction on your account.

## Internet Banking Safety

- Use a strong, unique password (mix of letters, numbers, symbols).
- Change your password periodically.
- Always log out after every session — especially on shared or public computers.
- Never use public Wi-Fi for banking. Use mobile data or a trusted home network.
- Check that the website address begins with **https://** and shows a lock icon.
- Do not use the same password for banking as for social media or email.

## Mobile Banking Safety

- Lock your phone with a strong PIN, pattern, or biometric lock.
- Install banking apps only from official sources (Google Play or Apple App Store).
- Do not root or jailbreak your phone if you use banking apps.
- Enable remote wipe in your phone settings so you can erase it if lost or stolen.
- If your SIM card suddenly stops working, call your mobile operator — you may be a SIM swap fraud victim.

## SIM Swap Fraud

Fraudsters can sometimes convince a mobile operator to issue a new SIM with your number, then receive your OTPs. Signs: your phone shows "No network" or "SIM not registered" unexpectedly.

If this happens, immediately call your mobile operator and your bank to block transactions.

## Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Enable 2FA on all accounts where it is available — email, social media, and banking. It adds a second layer of verification beyond just a password.

## What to Do if Your Account is Compromised

1. Immediately call your bank and block internet banking access.
2. Change your internet banking password from a safe device.
3. Call 1930 (National Cybercrime Helpline) to report the fraud.
4. File a complaint at https://cybercrime.gov.in.
