# Savings Basics — FD, RD, and PPF

## Why Save?

Saving money regularly — even small amounts — builds financial security over time. The key principle is to start early, because compound interest means early savings grow more than late savings.

## Fixed Deposit (FD)

A Fixed Deposit is a savings product offered by banks and post offices where you deposit a lump sum for a fixed period at a fixed interest rate.

**Key features:**
- Your money is locked in for the chosen period (7 days to 10 years).
- You earn a fixed interest rate that does not change during the tenure.
- Interest rates vary by bank, tenure, and RBI policy — check your bank's current rates.
- Premature withdrawal is usually allowed but may attract a penalty.
- FDs up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank are covered by DICGC insurance.

**Best for:** Short to medium-term goals where you do not need the money immediately.

## Recurring Deposit (RD)

A Recurring Deposit lets you save a fixed amount every month for a chosen period, earning interest similar to an FD.

**Key features:**
- Great for people with regular income who can save a fixed amount monthly.
- Available at banks and post offices.
- At maturity, you receive the total deposits plus accumulated interest.
- Interest rates vary — check your bank's current RD rates.

**Best for:** Building a savings habit with small regular amounts.

## Public Provident Fund (PPF)

PPF is a long-term savings and investment scheme backed by the Government of India.

**Key features:**
- Tenure: 15 years (extendable in blocks of 5 years).
- The interest rate is set by the government and reviewed periodically — check the current rate on the India Post or government website.
- Tax benefits: contributions qualify for deduction under Section 80C; interest and maturity amount are tax-free.
- Partial withdrawal allowed after 7 years under certain conditions.
- Loan against PPF allowed after 3 years.

**Best for:** Long-term goals (retirement, children's education) with tax savings.

## Comparing the Three

| Feature | FD | RD | PPF |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum tenure | 7 days | 6 months | 15 years |
| Lump sum or monthly | Lump sum | Monthly | Flexible (within limits) |
| Tax benefit | No (except 5-year tax-saver FD) | No | Yes (Section 80C) |
| Government guarantee | Up to ₹5L (DICGC) | Up to ₹5L (DICGC) | Full government backing |
| Liquidity | High | Medium | Low |

## General Savings Tips

- Start with an emergency fund before long-term savings.
- Do not keep all savings in a single bank or product.
- Review your savings plan once a year.
- Do not withdraw savings for non-emergency wants — let them compound.
