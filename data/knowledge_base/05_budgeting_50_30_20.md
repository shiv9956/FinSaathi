# Budgeting Basics and the 50/30/20 Rule

## Why Budget?

A budget is simply a plan for your money. Without a plan, money tends to disappear without meeting your actual goals. A budget helps you:
- Pay bills on time without stress.
- Build savings gradually.
- Avoid borrowing for everyday expenses.
- Work toward goals like buying a phone, a vehicle, or a home.

## The 50/30/20 Rule

This is one of the simplest budgeting frameworks:

| Category | Percentage | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Needs** | 50% | Rent, food, electricity, transport, medicine, school fees |
| **Wants** | 30% | Eating out, entertainment, subscriptions, shopping |
| **Savings** | 20% | Emergency fund, RD, PPF, loan repayment |

**Example:** Monthly income ₹25,000
- Needs: ₹12,500
- Wants: ₹7,500
- Savings: ₹5,000

Adjust percentages to your situation — if you have a low income, saving 20% may not be possible right away. Start with whatever you can, even ₹500 per month.

## Emergency Fund

An emergency fund is savings set aside for unexpected expenses — job loss, medical emergency, major repair.

**Target:** 3 to 6 months of your essential expenses.

Keep your emergency fund in a separate savings account or a short-term FD so it is accessible but not mixed with daily spending money.

**Why it matters:** Without an emergency fund, any unexpected expense becomes a debt. With one, you can handle emergencies without borrowing.

## How to Start Budgeting

1. Track your spending for one month (write every expense in a notebook or use a free budgeting app).
2. Categorise expenses into Needs, Wants, and Savings.
3. Identify where you can reduce Wants.
4. Set up automatic transfers to savings on salary day — "pay yourself first."
5. Review every month and adjust.

## Common Budgeting Mistakes to Avoid

- **Not including irregular expenses** (annual insurance premium, festival shopping). Divide these by 12 and save monthly.
- **Ignoring small expenses** — ₹50 here and ₹100 there add up quickly.
- **Treating credit card limits as income** — credit is not your money; it is a loan.
- **Stopping savings when expenses rise** — reduce Wants first, not Savings.
